Product case study · Harshit Yadav

IXL Analysis

Where IXL creates friction for students, teachers, and parents, and a feature design that lets teachers bundle skills, lessons, and videos into one homework assignment.

RoleSolo, end to end
FormatProduct analysis and feature design
UsersStudents, teachers, parents
OutputSix ideas, one feature spec, wireframes, data model
Summary

IXL started as a skills practice platform and has since added lessons and videos. But teachers still assign those separately, so the new content doesn't really fit into their homework workflow. I proposed Homework Assignments: one place to package skills, lessons, and videos, send them to a class, and track completion automatically.

The key decision was where the feature lives. I compared three entry points and chose a dedicated section in My IXL, next to Quizzes, because teachers already know that "create, assign, come back to review" pattern. I kept the MVP to that one entry point so IXL gets a clean signal on whether teachers create assignments and come back to monitor them.

Starting point

How I'd explain IXL to a teacher

Think of it like having a virtual teaching assistant for each student. It shows you what each student knows and where they're getting stuck, so they can keep practicing at their own pace, and you can turn everyday practice into a plan for class time.

Discovery

Six opportunities across three users

Before going deep on one feature, I looked for friction for each type of user. For every idea I wrote down what could go wrong, since most of these change how students feel about practice.

Students

Progressive explanations

ProblemFrustrated or rushed students skip the detailed explanation after a wrong answer, even when it would help.
IdeaShow a short, bullet style explanation first, with "Show me more" to expand. How often a student expands and still misses similar questions can hint at a conceptual gap versus a careless error.
TradeoffShort versions can oversimplify or encourage skimming. The conceptual vs careless signal needs testing, since it could produce false positives.

Restart or validate on stages you can't pass

ProblemIn staged science and social studies challenges, students who can no longer pass a stage are still made to finish it, and the goal shifts from learning to just finishing.
IdeaIf a student is close to the threshold, give one validation question on the missed concept. If passing is already out of reach, offer "Restart" or "Continue".
TradeoffIt could reduce productive persistence. Restarts need limits so students can't game them.

Goal met, keep practicing

ProblemSmartScore gets harder at the top, so students stop once they hit the teacher's target, afraid of losing points.
IdeaMark the assignment complete once the goal is reached (say 87 against a goal of 85), even if the score dips with more practice. The SmartScore algorithm stays the same.
TradeoffStudents may stop caring about mistakes after completion. Teachers would need both the highest and current score.
Teachers

Spaced retention checks

ProblemSmartScore shows performance at one moment. It doesn't show whether a skill is retained over time or applied when mixed with other skills.
IdeaShort periodic checks on skills a student hasn't practiced in a while, or mixed in with newer ones. The results feed the existing recommendations and intervention tools.
TradeoffHigh engineering effort with new scheduling logic, and less time for new skills.

Create intervention with Spark

ProblemAnalytics show the misconceptions, but teachers still spend a lot of time turning them into a plan, using context about their students that IXL doesn't have.
IdeaA "Create intervention" action from Trouble Spots that uses Spark Studio. The teacher adds guidance like "use visual examples" and gets a short reteach, examples, or discussion prompts. After reviewing it, students practice the matching IXL skill in Group Jam.
TradeoffGenerated content can be wrong, so teacher review is required. A quick bump after Group Jam may not mean lasting learning.
Parents

Learning Health

ProblemParents see lots of metrics but can't tell productive struggle from unproductive struggle. A long session can look good even when a child is missing question after question.
IdeaFlag patterns like repeated errors, falling accuracy, or rereading explanations without improving. Use neutral language ("Extended struggle detected") with a plain summary, like "good at finding slope, struggling to use it to find the equation of a line".
TradeoffSome students enjoy long sessions, and a break breaks momentum. Students may feel watched and rush to avoid alerts.
Deep dive

Homework Assignments

IXL now has lessons and videos alongside skills, but a teacher who wants students to watch a video, read a lesson, then practice has to coordinate those pieces separately. The goal is to bring that fragmented workflow into IXL: package the pieces into one clearly defined assignment, and make it obvious to students what to learn, do, and finish.

For teachers, it should

  • Be fast to create, mixing any skill, lesson, or video
  • Allow an optional order, like lesson first, then practice
  • Make expectations explicit: recipients, due date, instructions, SmartScore goals
  • Give one view of who hasn't started, who's in progress, who's done or late
  • Use one consistent rule for what "complete" means for each content type

For students, it should

  • Live in one place, clearly separate from optional practice
  • Show what's due, when, in what order, and what counts as done
  • Keep all the pieces together with progress like "2 of 4 done"
  • Track completion automatically, with no manual submission
Key decision

Where should it live?

I judged each entry point against the goals above: creation stays easy, mixed content feels like one assignment, and teachers come back to the same place to monitor it.

Option 1

Teacher Dashboard

Teachers already use it to see assigned and suggested skills.

Why not: Suggested Skills is built around single skill recommendations. A homework assignment has a title, recipients, due date, an ordered mix of content, and fixed results. Putting both there mixes two jobs and changes a workflow teachers rely on.

Chosen

New section in My IXL, next to Quizzes

Quizzes already set the mental model: create something, assign it, come back to review results. Homework isn't an assessment, but it behaves the same way, so teachers don't have to learn new navigation.

Builder: Add Skill, Add Lesson, Add Video, all inside the page, with related lessons and videos suggested after adding a skill.

Option 3

"Add to Homework" on the Learning page

Lowest friction, and closest to how teachers assign today by starring or sharing links.

Why not first: It mixes exploring with assigning, the new feature is easy to miss, and it still needs a separate place to monitor and edit.

MVPLaunch only the dedicated section. Besides smaller engineering scope, one entry point gives a clean signal on whether teachers create assignments and return to monitor and review them.
LaterIf teachers use it often and tend to find content while browsing Learning, add an "Add to Homework" action there that drops a resource into a new or existing assignment.

Under the hood

Data model

I kept the assignment definition separate from each student's progress. The teacher's expectations stay stable, and each student moves through the same assignment independently.

AssignmentID, creator, title, instructions, due date, class or students, completion rule, edit version
Assignment itemsType plus a reference to existing IXL content (never a copy), completion rule, position in the sequence
RecipientsClass or group, with a snapshot of students at publish time, since rosters change
Student assignment statusPer student and per item: not started, in progress, or complete, with start and finish times
Item progressSkills: SmartScore at start and end. Videos and lessons: watched or read percentage. Feeds the teacher's summary view.
Design choiceRecord the SmartScore when the assignment is published, not only at completion. A student may have practiced that skill before, and unrelated later practice shouldn't rewrite the assignment's result.
Design choiceKeep an edit history with the version each student received, so adding or removing an item mid assignment doesn't disturb work students already finished.
